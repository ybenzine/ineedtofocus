# I Need To Focus — Product Decisions

Status: agreed MVP product scope. The technical plan lives in [tech_stack.md](tech_stack.md).

Production: https://ineedtofoc.us · Dev: https://dev.ineedtofoc.us

## 1. Problem

People who want to do focused work, study, read, meditate, exercise or nap need three things that are usually spread across separate apps: a timer that fits how they work (fixed countdown, Pomodoro, or open-ended), calm background sound that keeps them in the zone, and an honest record of where their focus time actually went. **I Need To Focus** puts all three in one quiet, mobile-friendly app: start a session in a couple of taps, let it run with rain or a YouTube track in the background, and later review your focus history by day, week, month, all time or any custom range, filtered by what you were focusing on.

## 2. Users and platforms

- **Primary user:** an individual who wants to track their own focus time across devices. No teams or sharing in the MVP.
- **MVP platform:** a mobile-first web app that works in desktop and mobile browsers and can be installed to the home screen.
- **Stretch (post-MVP):** native iOS and Android apps.

### Known platform limitations (web MVP)

- Mobile browsers, iOS in particular, may pause timers and sound when the screen locks or the user switches apps. Timers are based on start/end timestamps so the time shown is always correct when the app comes back, but chimes and background audio may not play while the phone is locked. The "Keep screen awake" toggle (F9) mitigates this; reliable locked-screen alerts are a reason for the native stretch goal.
- YouTube's terms require a visible player and forbid extracting audio, and mobile browsers stop YouTube playback in the background. YouTube audio therefore plays through a small visible embedded player and works best on desktop or with the screen on.

## 3. Key user journey

> Sign in → choose a mode, tag and (optionally) a goal and background sound → start → focus → session ends → the session appears in History and in the totals.

This journey is the one that must never silently fail: **a finished session must never be lost.** That promise covers sessions of an account; a session finished without an account (AC1.8) is kept only if the user saves it from its summary, and from then on the promise covers it too.

## 4. MVP feature specification

Each feature lists acceptance criteria (AC) that define "done".

### F1. Accounts

Users sign up and sign in with email and password; their sessions and settings are stored on the server and available on any device. Without an account the timers still work, with nothing kept beyond the device (AC1.8).

- AC1.1 A user can sign up with an email and a password of 8 to 128 characters; duplicate emails (matched regardless of case and surrounding spaces) are rejected with a clear message.
- AC1.2 A user can sign in and sign out; a signed-out user cannot see or change any account data (using the app without an account, AC1.8, only touches data on the device).
- AC1.3 A user only ever sees their own sessions and settings.
- AC1.4 Signing in on a second device shows the same history and settings.
- AC1.5 Passwords are never displayed, logged, or stored in plain text.
- AC1.6 **Export my data** (in an Account section of Settings) downloads the whole account as one JSON file, `ineedtofocus-export-<YYYY-MM-DD>.json` (UTC date of the export): the account's email and creation time, its settings (the calendar settings of AC12.8 included, but never the calendar link, its secret or its hash, AC14.4), **every** session (archived ones included, with every stored field, oldest first, timestamps in UTC ISO 8601), its custom tags (removed ones included, with name, colour and when each was created and removed; AC7.4) and its recent YouTube links. It never contains the password hash, sign-ins or another user's data. The app tries to sync first (as at sign-out, at most 10 s); sessions or edits still unsynced on this device are counted in a note ("N sessions on this device haven't synced yet and aren't in the export."). Offline, nothing is downloaded and the button explains "Export needs a connection." (#349)
- AC1.7 **Delete account** (in the same Account section) asks for an inline confirmation that names what goes ("This permanently deletes your account, your N sessions (archived ones included), your settings, tags and recent YouTube links, on every device. This can't be undone."; with no sessions the sessions clause is left out; "tags" (AC7.4) is always there, like "settings"), links to Export my data and requires the account's password. It is refused while a timer is running or paused ("End your session before deleting your account."). A wrong password shows "Incorrect password" and deletes nothing; repeated wrong passwords count against the failed sign-in limit (5 per client and email, 20 per client, within 15 minutes). Nothing on this device is cleared unless the server confirms the deletion: a wrong password, a refusal for too many attempts, a network error or a server error leaves everything in place (F13: a finished session is never lost). The right password permanently deletes the account and everything stored with it on the server; once the server confirms, the app signs this device out and clears every app-owned store for that user, unsynced sessions included, then shows "Your account was deleted." on the sign-in screen. The next sign-in or sign-up in that tab opens New session, whatever screen was open before, and any sign-up does; a sign-in or sign-up from AC1.8's save prompt returns to its summary instead. Other devices signed in to the account get the expired-sign-in behaviour on their next request. The same email can then sign up again as a new, empty account. The deletion is logged only with the opaque user id; no email, password, goal or YouTube link reaches telemetry (§5). (#349, #357)
- AC1.8 **Without an account** (#331), anyone can use the app on their device: Countdown, Pomodoro and Unlimited with their alerts, Rain, YouTube (without recent links) and Settings, which are then saved on that device only, with a note saying so. History and Archive ask them to create an account or log in instead of showing data, and so does calendar export (F14), which is for signed-in users only (AC12.8, AC14.7). Using the app without an account creates no server-side user and calls none of the server's data endpoints. When a session without an account ends with at least 1 minute of focus (AC6.2), the summary says "Create an account or log in to save this session", with a link for each; signing up or logging in from it saves that session to the account through the normal sync (AC13.4). Nothing else done without an account is ever added to one: starting another session, leaving the summary, reloading or closing the page, or anyone signing in on the device other than from the prompt discards the session without a warning, and every sign-in clears the device's data from use without an account. Details in the Decision log (2026-10-30, #331).

### F2. Countdown timer

- AC2.1 The user can pick a duration from 1 to 240 whole minutes; values outside that range cannot be started.
- AC2.2 The timer shows remaining time and counts down to 00:00.
- AC2.3 At 00:00 the end bell plays and the session is saved with status **Completed**.
- AC2.4 The user can pause and resume; paused time does not count toward focus time.
- AC2.5 The user can stop early; the session is saved with status **Ended early** and its actual focus time (see F6).
- AC2.6 **Setup** (#405). A new Countdown setup starts at **30 min**. A new setup begins when the app loads and after each session; a setup in progress is kept when the user goes to another screen and back in the same tab (AC7.10). Signing in, signing up, signing out or deleting the account also begins a new setup (AC1.2, AC1.8). While a kept setup differs from a new one, the tab isn't idle for the automatic update (2026-10-25, #244), whichever screen it is on. Five presets, **15, 30, 45, 60 and 90**, set the length in one tap, and the one equal to the length shows as selected. **−** and **+** move to the next lower or higher multiple of 5, clamped to 1–240 (23 → 20 or 25; 1 → + → 5; 5 → − → 1). Typing a whole number of minutes still works, under AC2.1. Under the dial, the **summary line** gives the planned length, then the end time:
  - **Length:** under 60 minutes, `<m>m` (`30m`). From 60 on, `<h>h <m>m` (`1h 55m`), or just `<h>h` on a whole hour (`1h`, `4h`). There is no space between a number and `h` or `m`.
  - **End time:** the current time plus the length, refreshed at least once a minute while the screen is open, with no date even when it falls on the next day. It follows the hour cycle of the browser's locale as `Intl` reports it (a web page can't read the system's 12/24-hour toggle itself). In 12-hour form it has no leading zero and a lowercase `am` or `pm` after a space (`2:35 pm`); in 24-hour form it is written as the device's locale writes it (`14:35`).
  - **Together:** `<length> → <end time>`, as in `30m → 2:35 pm` or `1h 30m → 15:35`.
- AC2.7 **Recent custom time** (#405). When a Countdown starts with a length that isn't one of the presets, that length becomes the recent custom time. The preset row then shows it as a sixth button after 90, in the same style plus a small "recent" clock icon so it reads as the user's own; choosing it sets the length like a preset, and it shows as selected whenever the length equals it. A newer custom length replaces it. Starting on a preset, or in Pomodoro or Unlimited, leaves it as it is. A signed-in user's recent custom time belongs to the account and syncs like the other settings (AC12.7). Without an account it is kept on the device only, cleared with the rest of the device's data from use without an account and never added to an account (AC1.8). With no recent custom time the row has the five presets.

### F3. Pomodoro timer

- AC3.1 Defaults: 25 min focus, 5 min short break, 4 focus blocks, long break off.
- AC3.2 Configurable: focus 5–90 min, short break 1–30 min, focus blocks 1–8, optional long break 5–60 min (default 15) that replaces the short break after every 4th focus block. The user's last-used configuration is remembered.
- AC3.3 Phases advance automatically; the end bell plays at every automatic phase change (not when the user skips a break or stops early). There is no break after the final focus block.
- AC3.4 The screen always shows the current phase (Focus / Break / Long break), its remaining time, and progress (e.g. "Block 2 of 4").
- AC3.5 The user can pause/resume at any point and skip the current break.
- AC3.6 The whole Pomodoro is saved as **one** history entry. Only focus time counts toward totals; the entry also records how many focus blocks were completed.
- AC3.7 Stopping before the last block saves the entry as **Ended early** with the focus time accumulated so far.
- AC3.8 **Setup** (#405). The dial shows the focus length (−/+ within 5–90), with Break (−/+ by 1 within 1–30), Blocks (−/+ by 1 within 1–8) and the Long break switch ("every 4th block"; when on, its length −/+ within 5–60) underneath. AC3.2's ranges and whole-minute granularity are unchanged: the focus length and the long break can also be typed, like the Countdown length (AC2.6), so a value such as 52 or 17 can still be set and is remembered as it is (AC12.5). Their −/+ snap to the next lower or higher multiple of 5, as in AC2.6 (52 → 50 or 55; 5 → − stays 5). The summary line (AC2.6's format) gives the length of the whole session: every focus block plus every break between them, long breaks included, with no break after the last block (AC3.3). Four blocks of 25 with 5-minute breaks read `1h 55m → 4:10 pm`.

### F4. Unlimited timer

- AC4.1 The timer counts up from 00:00 until the user stops it.
- AC4.2 Optional reminder interval: Off (default), 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 or 120 min. At each interval a gentle chime (not a voice) plays.
- AC4.3 Pause/resume is supported; reminders are based on focus time, not wall-clock time.
- AC4.4 Stopping saves the session as **Completed**.
- AC4.5 The timer stops itself at 12 hours of focus time (paused time excluded) and saves the session as **Completed**, so a forgotten timer cannot run indefinitely.
- AC4.6 **Setup** (#405). The reminder choices of AC4.2 (Off, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 120) sit under the dial as one row headed "Remind me every", with the Settings default reminder (AC12.4) preselected; a new user's default stays Off. The dial shows ∞ and "no time limit", then "chime every <interval>" in AC2.6's length format (`chime every 30m`, `chime every 1h 30m`), or "no reminders" when the reminder is Off.

### F5. Sounds

- AC5.1 Two distinct, gentle sounds: an interval **chime** (F4 reminders) and an end **bell** (phase/session end). No voice.
- AC5.2 Separate volume controls for alert sounds and for background audio.
- AC5.3 Settings let the user preview each sound.

### F6. Session rules (all modes)

- AC6.1 Only one session can be active at a time.
- AC6.2 Sessions with less than 1 minute of focus time are not saved; the user is told why.
- AC6.3 Every saved session records: mode, tag, optional goal, start time, end time, focus time, paused time, status (Completed / Ended early), and the mode settings used.
- AC6.4 An active session survives a page reload or the app being closed and reopened on the same device, and shows the correct time on return.
- AC6.5 If a countdown or Pomodoro reaches its end while the app is closed, it is recorded as Completed at its scheduled end time.

### F7. Tags and goal

- AC7.1 Every session has exactly one tag: one of the six built-in tags **Work, Study, Nap, Read, Meditation, Exercise**, or one of the user's own tags (AC7.4).
- AC7.2 The tag defaults to the last one used (Work for a new user, and Work when the last one used has since been removed, AC7.7).
- AC7.3 The user can optionally enter a one-line goal ("What are you focusing on?"), up to 120 characters, shown on the timer screen and in history. Line breaks in a goal are replaced by a single space.
- AC7.4 **Custom tags** (#40). A signed-in user can create up to **20** tags of their own besides the six built-in ones, in a **Tags** section of Settings. They belong to the account, sync to every device like the other settings (AC1.4) and are never seen by another account (AC1.3). A name is trimmed, has line breaks replaced by a single space (as goals, AC7.3) and must be **1 to 24 characters**. It must differ, ignoring case, from the six built-in names and from every other custom tag of the account, removed ones included, so no two tags of an account ever share a name. A clash with a built-in tag or a tag in use shows "A tag with this name already exists." and saves nothing. Renaming a tag to a removed tag's name shows "A removed tag has this name. Add a tag with this name to bring it back." and saves nothing; adding a tag with that name brings the removed one back instead (AC7.7). With 20 tags in use, adding one, or bringing a removed one back, shows "You can have up to 20 tags of your own. Remove one to add another." An account keeps at most 100 custom tags, removed ones included; past that, adding a new one shows "You've reached the limit of 100 tags, including removed ones." Renaming or recolouring a tag that another device has just removed shows "This tag was removed on another device." and reloads the list. Bringing a removed tag back makes no new tag, so the 100 limit doesn't stop it.
- AC7.5 **Colours.** Every tag has one colour from a fixed palette of 8: **Indigo, Blue, Teal, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red, Pink**. There is no free colour input. Each palette colour has a value for the dark theme and one for the light theme, and each reaches at least 3:1 contrast against the page background and the card surface of its theme. The timer screen and the session summary, which stay dark in every mode (AC9.1), use the dark values even when the light theme is on. The built-in tags have fixed colours: Work Indigo, Study Blue, Nap Pink, Read Orange, Meditation Teal, Exercise Green. A new tag's colour choice starts on the first palette colour, in palette order, that no tag in use (built-in, or custom and not removed) has, or on Indigo if all are taken. Colours may repeat.
- AC7.6 **Rename and recolour.** A custom tag can be renamed and given another colour at any time, under the rules of AC7.4 and AC7.5. The change shows at once on every session that has the tag, past ones included, and on other devices after their next settings load. The six built-in tags can't be renamed, recoloured, removed or hidden: the Tags section lists them first, with their dots and no controls, under the note "Built-in tags can't be changed."
- AC7.7 **Remove.** Removing a custom tag asks for an inline confirmation ("Remove <name>? It leaves the tag picker. Sessions that have it keep it."). A removed tag leaves the setup picker and the Tags list in Settings; no session changes, and History, the summary and Archive keep showing it with its name and colour. The History tag filter still lists it, after the tags in use, marked "(removed)". A session being edited (AC11.1) keeps a removed tag until another is picked, but a removed tag can't be picked again. A running timer keeps its tag and is saved with it. When the last-used tag is removed, the next setup starts on Work. Adding a tag with the name of a removed one (ignoring case; names are unique per account, AC7.4, so at most one matches) brings that tag back, with its sessions, its stored name (as it was spelled, whatever case was just typed) and the colour just picked, instead of making a new one; it then counts toward the 20 in use again. Nothing deletes a custom tag permanently except deleting the account (AC1.7).
- AC7.8 **Where colours show.** A small colour dot sits before the tag's name in the setup picker (the Tag & goal sheet since #405, AC7.10), on the timer screen, on the session summary, in History (session list, tag breakdown, tag filter), in Archive and in the Settings Tags list. The dot is decorative: the name is always shown beside it, so colour is never the only way to tell tags apart. The History bar chart keeps its single colour. A control that can't show a dot (the History edit form's tag dropdown) shows the name only. Everywhere a list of tags is offered (the setup picker, the History edit form, the History tag filter and the Settings Tags list) they appear in one order: the six built-in ones in AC7.1's order, then custom tags oldest first (in the filter, removed ones after those in use, AC7.7). The History tag breakdown stays most focus first (AC10.3), with ties in that order. A tag this device doesn't know yet (a session synced from another device before this device has loaded that tag) shows as "Tag" with no dot until the next settings load, and never breaks a screen. A 24-character name fits at 360 px wide without horizontal scrolling.
- AC7.9 **Offline, without an account, and privacy.** Creating, renaming, recolouring and removing a tag needs a connection; offline, the Tags section shows the list without its controls and says "Changing tags needs a connection." Picking a tag, and starting, finishing, editing and syncing sessions with it, work offline as before (F13). Without an account (AC1.8) only the six built-in tags exist; the Tags section shows them with "Create an account or log in to add your own tags." Custom tag names are user content like goals: they never reach logs, traces or metrics, and error responses never echo them (§5).
- AC7.10 **Tag & goal sheet** (#405). On the New session screen the tag and the goal are picked in one bottom sheet, titled "Tag & goal", opened from the Tag row or the Goal row; each row shows its current value ("Add a goal" when there is none). The sheet is the setup picker of AC7.7 and AC7.8, so their rules carry over unchanged from #401's picker: tags show as pills with their colour dot, the six built-in ones in AC7.1's order then the account's custom tags oldest first; removed tags aren't offered; the default is the last-used tag, or Work when it has been removed (AC7.2, AC7.7); a 24-character name fits at 360 px. The pills form a single-choice group that wraps and scrolls when long. A **Manage tags** link opens Settings → Tags; it shows only to a signed-in user, since without an account there are only the six built-in tags (AC7.9). Coming back to New session afterwards, through the tab bar or the browser's Back, finds the setup as it was left (mode, lengths, reminder, tag, goal and sound), as for any trip to another screen in the same tab (AC2.6); a tag just created there isn't selected automatically. The goal field, labelled "Goal" and marked optional, keeps AC7.3's rules: the prompt "What are you focusing on?" as its placeholder, 120 characters with a `0/120` counter, line breaks replaced by a space. **Done**, Escape or a tap outside the sheet closes it with the choices kept.

### F8. Background audio

- AC8.1 Before or during a session the user picks one source: **None**, **Rain** or **YouTube**.
- AC8.2 Audio starts with the session, pauses when the timer pauses, and stops when the session ends. In a Pomodoro session it also pauses for each break (short and long) and resumes when the next focus block starts, unless the user has turned off *Pause background audio during Pomodoro breaks* (AC12.6); then it keeps playing through breaks.
- AC8.3 **Rain:** a loop that sounds like real rain: a recording owned by the project (since #135, Yacine's own recording of light rain), a verified CC0 recording, or an original generated work owned by the project; it loops seamlessly (no audible gap), works fully offline, and is small (30–60 s, ≤ 600 KB). Quality bar in the Decision log (#17).
- AC8.4 **YouTube:** the user pastes a YouTube link; the video plays in a small, visible embedded player and loops for as long as the session runs.
- AC8.5 Invalid links, unavailable videos and videos that do not allow embedding show a clear message; the session itself is unaffected. In a playlist, once an item has played, an item that can't be played is skipped; the message shows when the items fail in a row (details in the Decision log, #203).
- AC8.6 The 5 most recent YouTube links are remembered per user for one-tap reuse.
- AC8.7 When offline, YouTube is unavailable and the app suggests Rain.
- AC8.8 **Sound sheet** (#405). On the New session screen the background audio is picked in a bottom sheet of the same kind as the Tag & goal sheet (AC7.10), opened from the Sound row, which shows the choice with its icon. It offers None, Rain and YouTube; YouTube shows the link field and the recent links as before (AC8.4–AC8.7), and Start stays blocked until a chosen YouTube link can play (#325).

### F9. Timer screen

- AC9.1 A dark, distraction-free full-screen view showing the time, mode/phase, tag, goal and audio controls.
- AC9.2 "Keep screen awake" toggle that keeps the screen on during a session where the device supports it.
- AC9.3 Layout works on phone screens (from 360 px wide) as well as desktop.
- AC9.4 During a session the session controls (Pause or Resume, Stop, and Skip break during a Pomodoro break) sit directly below the tag and goal line and above the bottom area, which holds the background audio controls and then "Keep screen awake". The order is the same in every mode (Countdown, Pomodoro, Unlimited) and at every width from 360 px.
- AC9.5 **New session dial** (#405). The New session screen previews the session on a ring like the timer screen's, with the mode switch above it, the presets or reminder row below it, then the Tag, Goal and Sound rows; Start stays visible above the navigation on a phone. The ring reads like a clock, **60 minutes per lap**: a Countdown fills `minutes / 60` of a lap, and past 60 the full laps alternate between the accent and a lighter shade of it, with the current lap drawn on top (90 = an accent lap plus half a lighter lap; 150 = a lighter lap plus half an accent lap). A Pomodoro is one full lap split in proportion: focus blocks in the accent, breaks left as the unfilled track. Unlimited shows ∞ with a tick at the top (0) and at every further multiple of the reminder interval below 60: 15 gives 4 ticks (0, 15, 30, 45), 30 gives 2 (0, 30), 45 gives 2 (0, 45), and 60, 90 and 120 give 1, at the top; Off gives none. No ring arc has rounded ends. Unlike the timer screen (AC9.1), the dial follows the app theme. In both themes the accent and the lighter lap shade each reach at least 3:1 against the page background and against the ring's unfilled track, and differ from each other by at least 1.5:1 (in the light theme a shade with a relative luminance of about 0.18–0.25 meets all three). Reference: [design/new-session-mockup.html](design/new-session-mockup.html).

### F10. History

- AC10.1 Views: **Day, Week, Month, All time, Custom range** (start and end date, inclusive). Day/Week/Month views have previous/next navigation.
- AC10.2 Tag filter: select one or more tags, built-in or custom (removed ones included, AC7.7); a session is shown if its tag is any of the selected ones. Default: all tags.
- AC10.3 Each view shows total focus time, number of sessions, a breakdown of focus time by tag, a bar chart (by day for Week, Month and Custom ranges up to ~2 months; by month for All time and longer ranges), and the list of sessions, newest first.
- AC10.4 Each session in the list shows date, start time, mode, tag, focus time, status and goal.
- AC10.5 Days and weeks follow the user's local time zone; a session that crosses midnight counts toward the day it started.
- AC10.6 Weeks start on **Sunday** by default; configurable in Settings (F12).
- AC10.7 Totals and filters exclude archived sessions.
- AC10.8 A clear empty state when a view has no sessions.
- AC10.9 Sessions that are saved but not yet synced (F13) appear in history with a "not synced yet" indicator. A session the server rejected stays on the device with a separate "Couldn't sync" indicator.

### F11. Editing and archiving sessions

Sessions are archived instead of deleted, so nothing is lost by accident.

- AC11.1 For an active (non-archived) session, the user can change its tag (to any tag that isn't removed, AC7.7) and goal. Times and durations cannot be edited in the MVP.
- AC11.2 The user can archive a session; it disappears from history views and totals immediately, with an "Undo" option.
- AC11.3 An **Archive** view lists all archived sessions.
- AC11.4 Archived sessions cannot be modified; they can only be **restored** or **deleted**.
- AC11.5 Restoring returns the session to history exactly as it was.
- AC11.6 "Delete all archived sessions" permanently deletes every archived session after a confirmation dialog that states how many will be deleted. This cannot be undone.
- AC11.7 Non-archived sessions can never be permanently deleted directly.

### F12. Settings (per user, synced across devices)

- AC12.1 Week start day (default Sunday).
- AC12.2 Alert sound volume and background audio volume, each 0–100 (defaults: alert 80, background 50).
- AC12.3 Keep screen awake default (off for a new user).
- AC12.4 Default unlimited-timer reminder interval.
- AC12.5 Last-used Pomodoro configuration and tag are remembered (no stored tag means Work, per AC7.2).
- AC12.6 *Pause background audio during Pomodoro breaks*: on by default for every user, existing users included. When off, background audio keeps playing through Pomodoro breaks (the behaviour before this change).
- AC12.7 The recent custom Countdown time (AC2.7): 1–240 minutes, or none for a new user. It is stored with the account's settings and syncs like them; the server doesn't compare it with the presets.
- AC12.8 **Calendar settings** (F14, #380). They shape the events of all three ways of AC14.1 and sync like the other settings. Like all of F14 they are for signed-in users only: without an account, Settings → Calendar shows no settings, only "Create an account or log in to add your sessions to your calendar." with a link for each, as the Tags section does (AC7.9).
  - **Title template:** at most 120 characters, default `{tag}: {goal}`; rendered by AC14.3.
  - **Include goals:** default on. When off, `{goal}` is empty and the description has no Goal line.
  - **Include description:** default on. When off, events have no description.
  - **Tags in the calendar:** every tag is in by default, and so is any tag created later; the user unticks the ones to leave out. Settings lists the tags in the History filter's order (AC7.8), removed ones last and marked "(removed)". Choices follow the tag, not its name, so a renamed tag keeps its choice, and removing a tag or bringing it back (AC7.7) changes nothing. With every tag unticked the feed is empty, and Settings says "No tags are selected, so your calendar stays empty." This setting applies to the subscription feed only (AC14.5).
  - **Minimum length:** None, 5, 10 or 15 minutes of focus time (default None); sessions with less focus time are left out of the feed and the History download, not of the per-session button (AC14.7).
  - **Calendar name:** 1 to 60 characters after trimming, default "I Need To Focus".

### F13. Offline use and sync

- AC13.1 Once signed in, all three timers, Rain, and sound alerts work without a connection.
- AC13.2 A finished session is saved on the device immediately, before any network call.
- AC13.3 Unsynced sessions and changes are sent automatically when the connection returns.
- AC13.4 Retrying a sync never creates duplicate sessions.
- AC13.5 A finished session is never lost due to a network error or a server error.

F13 covers sessions of an account, including one saved from AC1.8's prompt once it is handed to the account; a session without an account is kept only by the page that shows its summary (Decision log 2026-10-30, #331).

### F14. Calendar export

Past focus sessions show up in the calendar apps people already use (Apple Calendar, Google Calendar, Outlook), next to the rest of their week. Everything is standard iCalendar (RFC 5545): there is no Google API and no sign-in to Google. Calendar export is for signed-in users only (Decision log 2026-11-02). Added after the MVP (#380, Yacine's decision of 2026-10-30), so it isn't part of §7's core MVP. The expected events are in [api/fixtures/calendar-events.json](api/fixtures/calendar-events.json), which the backend and frontend tests both read.

- AC14.1 **Three ways**, all built from the event format of AC14.2 and the settings of AC12.8:
  - **Subscription feed** (signed in): a private `webcal://` link, added once in a calendar app, where it shows as its own calendar and stays up to date (AC14.4, AC14.5). Apple's refresh interval is set by the user; Google refreshes on its own schedule, often several hours apart.
  - **History download** (signed in): an `.ics` file of the History view on screen, a snapshot (AC14.6). Google imports it only from calendar.google.com on a computer.
  - **Add to calendar for one session** (signed in), on the session summary and on each History row: a pre-filled Google Calendar link and a one-event `.ics` (AC14.7).
- AC14.2 **Event format.** One event per session, Pomodoro included: block and pause times aren't stored, so there are no per-block events. Archived sessions are never included.
  - **UID** `<session id>@ineedtofoc.us`, on dev too. Session ids are made on the device (AC13.4), so the app and the server give a session the same UID. Re-importing a file updates its events instead of duplicating them where the calendar app supports it, and the feed shows edits and drops archived sessions.
  - **Start and end:** `started_at` → `ended_at` in UTC (`20261030T090000Z`), to the whole second (fractions are dropped). This is the wall-clock span, pauses included; focus time is in the description.
  - **Title** from the title template (AC14.3).
  - **Description** (unless off, AC12.8), plain text, one item per line, in this order: `Goal: <goal>` (when there is a goal and goals are included), `Tag: <tag>`, `Mode: <Countdown|Pomodoro|Unlimited>`, `Focus time: <time>`, `Paused: <time>` (only when there was a pause, however short: `Paused: 40 s`), `Blocks completed: <n> of <blocks>` (Pomodoro only), `Ended early` (when so) and `History: <app origin>/history`. Times read as History shows them (`25 min`, `1 h 05 min`). The app origin is the address of the app that made the event (`https://ineedtofoc.us`, or the dev address on dev).
  - **Tag** wherever it appears (`{tag}`, the description, `CATEGORIES`) is the tag's display name: a built-in name, or a custom tag's current name (AC7.4), so a rename shows in the feed at its next refresh and a removed tag keeps its name (AC7.7). A tag the device hasn't loaded yet reads "Tag" (AC7.8).
  - **Goal** as stored, with any line break replaced by a single space (AC7.3), so it never breaks the one-item-per-line description.
  - **Show as free** (`TRANSP:TRANSPARENT`), `CATEGORIES` = the tag's display name, no alarms, no per-event colour.
  - **Calendar:** the name is the calendar name setting (AC12.8), with a suggested refresh of 1 hour, which Apple uses and Google ignores.
  - **File format:** `VERSION:2.0`, a `PRODID`, a `DTSTAMP` per event (when the file is made), CRLF line endings, TEXT values escaped (`\\`, `\;`, `\,`, `\n`) and lines folded at 75 octets, never inside a UTF-8 character. Folding acts on the escaped line, so a fold may fall between a backslash and the character it escapes. The app and the server fold lines themselves rather than through a library's serializer, which may fold differently (`icalendar` folds its first line at 74 octets); a library is used only to parse the output in tests. The fixture states the folding rule and gives each property's exact line.
- AC14.3 **Title template.** The placeholders are `{tag}`, `{goal}`, `{mode}` and `{focus_time}`, written exactly so. Each is replaced by its value in a single pass, so placeholder text inside a value (a goal reading "Plan the {mode} talk") stays as it is; an empty one (no goal, or goals off) becomes nothing. Then runs of spaces collapse to one, and spaces and the separators `·`, `:`, `-` and `–` left at either end are trimmed; separators in the middle stay. An empty result reads "Focus session". Any other `{…}` text stays as typed. The default `{tag}: {goal}` gives "Work: Write chapter 3", or "Work" with no goal.
- AC14.4 **Subscription link.** One link per account, made only on request (off by default) in Settings → Calendar. Its secret is 32 random bytes and only its SHA-256 hash is stored, like sign-in tokens, so **the full link is shown only once**: when it is made, and again after **Reset link**, which replaces it so the old one stops working at once. **Turn off** removes it. Anyone with the link can read the feed without signing in; Settings says so in plain words, naming goals when they are included. Settings shows when a calendar app last fetched the feed ("not yet" before the first fetch). An unknown, reset or turned-off link gets the same 404, never saying which. Deleting the account removes the link (AC1.7). The account export (AC1.6) has the calendar settings but never the link, the secret or its hash.
- AC14.5 **Feed content.** Every non-archived session of the account whose tag is in the calendar (AC12.8) and whose focus time meets the minimum length, newest first. Edits, archiving, restoring, the settings and custom tag renames show at the calendar app's next refresh.
- AC14.6 **History download.** **Add to calendar (.ics)** on History saves the sessions the view on screen counts (its Day, Week, Month, custom range or All time view, AC10.1, and its tag filter, AC10.2; days in local time, AC10.5; archived sessions left out, AC10.7), after the minimum length; the calendar's tag setting doesn't apply, since the History filter already chose. The file is `ineedtofocus-<first date>-to-<last date>.ics`, the first and last local dates of the view's range (not of the sessions in it; a Day view repeats its date), or `ineedtofocus-all.ics` for All time. Sessions not synced yet aren't in it, and the hint says so when this device has some. It needs a connection.
- AC14.7 **One session.** An **Add to calendar** control on the session summary and on each non-archived History row offers **Google Calendar** (a pre-filled event page in a new tab; it needs a connection) and **Apple Calendar / other (.ics)** (a one-event file, which works offline). The event is made on the device, so it works for sessions not synced yet and offline, and it matches the feed's event, UID included. The title template, Include goals and Include description apply; the tag and minimum-length settings don't, since the user picked this session. It is for signed-in users only: the summary of a session without an account (AC1.8) has no Add to calendar control and keeps its "Create an account or log in to save this session" prompt as it is.
- AC14.8 **Privacy.** Goals and custom tag names are user content (§5): neither they nor the link's secret ever reach logs, traces or metrics, and error responses never echo them.

## 5. Non-functional requirements (product-level)

- **Reliability:** saving a finished session and loading history are the critical paths (see §3).
- **Privacy:** session goals, custom tag names and email addresses are personal data; they must not appear in analytics, logs or monitoring.
- **Accessibility:** controls are usable with keyboard and screen readers; time is readable at a glance; respects reduced-motion settings.
- **Calm by design:** no streak shaming, no pop-ups during a session, no ads.

## 6. Out of scope for the MVP (post-MVP backlog)

- Export history as CSV (#38, separate from calendar export, F14)
- Writing to calendars through the Google Calendar API (F14 uses a subscription feed and files instead)
- Daily focus goal and streaks
- Manually adding a session (forgot to start the timer)
- Editing session times/durations
- Notifications when a session ends (including while the app is in the background)
- Password reset by email, Google sign-in
- Picking up an active session on a different device
- Multiple tags per session
- **Stretch:** native iOS and Android apps

## 7. Prioritization

If time runs short, cut in this order, first cut first: YouTube background audio (F8.4–F8.7) → Pomodoro long break → custom history range. Everything else in §4 is the core MVP.
